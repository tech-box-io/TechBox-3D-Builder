## 1. The stub colours are reversed in the model; the leads are right

### What the model draws

The buzzer is one mesh (`Piezo Buzzer`) with three solids: the body and two coloured stubs (`Surface-Offset1[1]` and `[2]`). The
atlas colours them (`colour-atlas-lac.json`, entry "Piezo Buzzer"; the materials.json rule `^Piezo Buzzer$` names no colour;
model.report.json says "atlas match 100%"). In the mesh's own frame the atlas has

| solid | own frame z | colour |
|---|---|---|
| Surface-Offset1[1] | -1.476 .. -0.048 | #ef3340 (red) |
| Surface-Offset1[2] | +0.048 .. +1.476 | #2d2926 (black) |

NAUO62 is placed the same way in lac-guide.glb (node 17) and in Cadasio: assembly x = 675.26 - own z, y = 919.65 + own x,
z = 1235.03 - own y. Seated, **the red stub is at x 675.31 - 676.74 (centre 676.03) and the black stub at x 673.79 - 675.21
(centre 674.50)**. Both stubs span z 1232.41 - 1233.84 and y 894.25 - 904.92: they hang straight down and end at y 894.24.

I checked this in the model the player loads. In model.glb, dequantised through node NAUO62's matrix, the #ef3340 primitive spans
x 675.32 - 676.74 and the #2d2926 primitive x 673.79 - 675.21. The colour matcher (tools/build_model.py `colour_from_atlas`) tries
every axis mapping and keeps the first best one. The two stubs are mirror images, so the identity mapping and the z-flip both score
100% and the identity wins. The model is coloured exactly as the atlas says.

### What guide.py draws

`BUZZER_LEADS` (guide.py:67) has red from [674.5, 894.24, 1233.12] to j4 (cap NAUO58) and black from [676.02, 894.24, 1233.12] to
(-) blue (cap NAUO59). So the drawn red lead leaves the black stub and the drawn black lead leaves the red stub, which is what Lee's
picture shows.

### Which is right

* **The CAD's leads** (wire-paths.json) agree with guide.py. With the buzzer clamped, NAUO65 runs from [674.50, 892.36, 1233.20]
  (the x 674.50 stub) to cap NAUO58 (j4), and NAUO64 runs from [676.04, 892.76, 1233.26] (the x 676.03 stub) to NAUO59 ((-) blue).
  Lying, NAUO60 ends at [650.84, 897.11, 1401.56] and NAUO63 at [650.84, 897.11, 1400.04]. BUZZER_LYING maps the seated x 674.5 exit
  exactly onto 1401.56 and the 676.02 exit onto 1400.04. NAUO60 goes to NAUO58 (j4) and NAUO63 to NAUO59. So in both poses the CAD
  wires **the x 674.5 stub (solid [2]) to j4 and the x 676.02 stub (solid [1]) to (-) blue**, and guide.py's exits and holes follow it.
* **TechBox's published Cadasio guide** (docs/research/lac/cadasio/raw, read per solid and per view with lac_atlas.py's rules) says
  red to j4 and black to (-) blue in Steps 33 and 34. Cadasio has the buzzer twice and colours the two copies differently:

  | Cadasio copy | shown in views | solid [1] | solid [2] | lead from [1] | lead from [2] |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Piezo Buzzer-1, lying (Steps 33 - 43) | 72 - 95 (20 views) | black | red | Part30 (NAUO63), black, to (-) blue | Part29 (NAUO60), red, to j4 |
  | Piezo Buzzer-2, clamped (title, 43c on) | 1 - 131 (37 views) | red | black | Part31 (NAUO64), red, to (-) blue | Part32 (NAUO65), black, to j4 |

  The lying copy is the one the published wiring steps show. It has the red stub and red lead going to j4, as the captions say and
  as guide.py does. The clamped copy has the stubs and its leads the other way round (red to the (-) blue rail). That is an
  inconsistency inside TechBox's own project, and it only shows once the buzzer is in the case.
* **Our atlas took the clamped copy** (`"cadasio_instance": "Piezo Buzzer-2"`, because lac_atlas.py keeps the first copy it meets).

### Fix: swap the two stub colours in the atlas (guide.py unchanged)

Swapping the exits in guide.py instead would put the red lead on the stub that the CAD wires to (-) blue. Every buzzer route

Alternatively red would go to (-) blue, which contradicts the captions and the buzzer's polarity.

## 2. The discontinuity in Step 43 onwards (lee_7)

Lee's picture lee_7 shows Step 43c: the F7s are still spread and the buzzer is seated. I measured where each drawn lead ends at the
buzzer against the end of its stub in every view (the routes in steps.json, mapped back to the seated frame):

| views | route | red: gap to stub end, angle to stub axis | black |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steps 33 - 43 (lying, lifted, over the seat) | 50 - 53, 67 - 70 | 0.00 mm, 0.0 deg | 0.00 mm, 0.0 deg |
| Step 43c to the Completed View (clamped) | 71, 72 | 1.88 mm (end [674.50, 892.36, 1233.20]), 40.2 deg | 1.49 mm ([676.04, 892.76, 1233.26]), 38.1 deg |

The cause is that `clamped_from_buzzer` / `buzzer_clamped` draw the CAD's clamped wire bodies (NAUO65 and NAUO64) as they are, and
the CAD starts each body 1.5 - 1.9 mm short of its stub and already bent towards +z. Three other things make it look like a hook:

* The CAD's clamped leads drop to y 889.85 (red, z 1244) and y 889.62 (black, z 1245). That is 2 mm above F3 (top 887.61) and
  7.3 mm below the breadboard's top (897.13), between the buzzer and the breadboard's end (z 1264.41).
* They then climb over the breadboard's end corner with 1.6 mm to spare (y 898.0 at z 1262.4).
* Step 43's over-the-seat shape (routes 68 and 70) has a V where it joins the CAD lead: bend radius 1.2 mm at [673.7, 902.1, 1263.1]
  and 1.1 mm at [675.9, 901.6, 1263.1].

**Fix:** the shapes in section 3 start at the stub's end. They go 1.0 mm straight on down (`BUZZER_SEAT_OUT`) and then turn under
the stub with a radius of 2.3 - 2.8 mm. The lowest point is y 891.3, a 2.9 mm dip; the stub points down, so some dip is unavoidable.
The gap is 0.00 mm and the angle 0 deg. The new over-the-seat shapes have no V: their tightest bend away from the hole is 4.4 mm.

**Fallback, only if section 3 is not taken.** This keeps the CAD's shape and joins it to the stub:

```python
def clamped_from_buzzer(cap, key, exit_):
    """The CAD's lead with the buzzer clamped, from the stub's end (straight on 1 mm, then smoothly onto the CAD's lead 6 mm along) to the
    cap, every 1 mm; and where it is BUZZER_J_MM along."""
    L = W.resample(cad_line(key, cap)[::-1], 1.0)
    e, d = np.asarray(exit_, float), np.array([0.0, -1.0, 0.0])
    head = W.smooth([e + d * 1.0, L[6]], start_dir=d, end_dir=unit(L[7] - L[6]), spacing=0.5)
    line = W.resample(W.join(W.straight(e, d, 1.0), head, L[6:]), 1.0)
    s = np.r_[0.0, np.cumsum(np.linalg.norm(np.diff(line, axis=0), axis=1))]
    return line, int(np.searchsorted(s, BUZZER_J_MM))
```

`buzzer_over_seat` and `buzzer_clamped` then call `clamped_from_buzzer(cap, key, exit_)` (both have the exit to hand). I checked it:
it starts at the stub's end along its axis, and it clears F3 by 1.39 mm and the buzzer by 1.4 mm. It does nothing for section 3: the
black lead still touches the blue jumper (-0.08 mm).

def seated_from_buzzer(colour, cap, exit_):
    """A buzzer lead seated, from the stub's end to the hole, every 1 mm (red in over a crest under the yellow jumper, as buzzer_line);
    and the index of the junction (BUZZER_J_Z)."""
    e, d = np.asarray(exit_, float), np.array([0.0, -1.0, 0.0])
    above = BUZZER_ABOVE[colour]
    top = wire_at(cap) + [0.0, 4.0 + above, 0.0]
    way = [np.asarray(p, float) for p in BUZZER_SEAT_WAY[colour]]
    if colour == 'red':
        h = top - way[-1]
        h[1] = 0.0
        way.append(top - unit(h) * 4.5 + [0.0, 1.5, 0.0])
    line = into_hole(cap, e, d, way, lift=BUZZER_SEAT_OUT, above=above)
    return line, int(np.argmax(line[:, 2] >= BUZZER_J_Z))


def buzzer_over_seat(colour, cap, exit_):
    """A buzzer lead with the buzzer held over its seat (BUZZER_UP): from its exit through BUZZER_UP_WAY into the seated lead before
    BUZZER_J_Z, then along it to the hole. Drawn from the hole, the run from the hole to there held still (b_from) and kept (tail_from)
    as the buzzer is lowered onto its seat: only the run behind the buzzer moves."""
    L, j = seated_from_buzzer(colour, cap, exit_)
    e, d = np.add(exit_, BUZZER_UP), np.array([0.0, -1.0, 0.0])
    body = W.smooth([e + d * BUZZER_OUT, *[np.asarray(c, float) for c in BUZZER_UP_WAY[colour]], L[j]], start_dir=d,
                    end_dir=unit(L[j + 2] - L[j]), spacing=0.5)
    line = W.resample(W.join(W.straight(e, d, BUZZER_OUT), body, L[j:]), 1.0)[::-1]
    k = shared_to(line, L[j])
    return W.route(line, a=cap, b=BUZZER, b_at=BUZZER_UP, b_from=k, tail_from=k)


def buzzer_clamped(colour, cap, exit_):
    """A buzzer lead seated (seated_from_buzzer), drawn from the hole; the run from the hole to the junction held still and kept."""
    L, j = seated_from_buzzer(colour, cap, exit_)
    line = L[::-1]
    k = shared_to(line, L[j])
    return W.route(line, a=cap, b=BUZZER, b_from=k, tail_from=k)

### What is still not done: the carry beat in Step 43

The lifted shape (BUZZER_LIFT_WAY: the buzzer 60 mm over where it lies, past the breadboard's +z end) leaves the holes towards +z. The
shape over the seat leaves them towards -z along the seated run. The player moves each point of a lead in a straight line from one
shape to the other, matched by share of length because route 67 is `stretch`. So the middle of each lead crosses the board through
whatever lies between, for about a third of a second, as it does today.

A clean carry needs every Step 43 shape to leave the holes the same way, and to stay above everything except a short run near the
