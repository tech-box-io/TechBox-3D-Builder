## 0. What this stretch is, in one paragraph

Three things happen. (a) The last two of the three screws that hang the claw on the arm go in: the second one on the +Z side
(carry-over from stretch 2) and the single one on the -Z side, for which the robot is turned end for end. (b) The claw servo's
lead is threaded back through the arm: in through the elbow-end square hole of A6, down A6's engraved face, out through the
shoulder-end square hole, down into the turret. (c) The lead is joined to the extension lead (twisted, blue flag "10") in a
black coupler sleeve, the extension's other end is plugged into the shield, and the join is pushed down inside the turret behind
R4 with the extension's wires dropped into R4's slot. Nothing is powered: no USB, no battery, no servo runs, no horn is touched.
The video ends with the finished robot being lifted and shown.

## 4. Hard to show in a 3D guide, or where the video and the CAD differ

1. **Threading has to come before the join.** The servo plug is 14.0 x 7.9 x 2.8 mm (CAD NAUO90); the holes in A6 are 8.0 x 8.0,
   the hole in C3 is 6.3 x 10.0. The plug only goes through flat-ways and with a wiggle (about 10 s per hole on video). The
   coupler sleeve is 10.0 mm wide and cannot pass an 8 mm hole at all. So the order is forced: C3 hole (during claw assembly),
   A6 elbow hole, A6 shoulder hole, and only then the coupler. A guide that fits the lead as one finished object would be wrong.
2. **Direction through each hole of A6.** Elbow-end hole: from the back (the face away from the controller) to the engraved
   face. Shoulder-end hole: from the engraved face to the back. Between the holes the lead is on the ENGRAVED face. The CAD path
   NAUO94 does the same: A6's engraved face looks along [-0.74, 0.67, 0]; the path passes the elbow-end hole heading
   (-0.95, 0.32, 0), which is towards the engraved side, and the shoulder-end hole heading (0.22, -0.98, 0), which is away from it.
3. **Arm pose.** The builder stands the upper arm upright and folds the forearm to reach the back of A6, and before that lays
   the arm out flat to drive the claw screws. The CAD has one pose. A guide step in the CAD pose will not show A6's back face
   or the route under the claw well.
4. **Where the join ends up.** CAD: plug NAUO90, coupler NAUO91 and plug NAUO92 lie in a straight HORIZONTAL line along -X at
   y = 133.6 (x 167.88 down to 138.88, z 140.7 to 148.6), with the claw lead first dropping "straight down just in front of R3"
   and the extension leaving through the POCKET of R4's slot. Video: the join stands roughly UPRIGHT just behind R4, the claw
   lead comes to it in one slack line from A6's lower hole, and what sits in R4's slot is the extension's three thin wires just
   below the lower plug. The coupler itself could not go through the slot (the mouth is 2.12 mm wide, the pocket 8.1 x 8.5).
5. **Order of the last two wiring actions.** On video the extension is plugged into the shield FIRST (6:44-6:50) and dropped
   into R4's slot and pushed into the turret AFTERWARDS (6:51-6:57). Either order works; the guide can choose.
6. **The coupler arrives on the extension lead.** No step on video shows the sleeve being pushed onto the extension's plug.
   If the kit ships them separate, that is one more step which this video does not cover. I could not see inside the sleeve
   (pins or not).
7. **Extension lead colours.** Twisted yellow / red / brown on video, where the servo leads are flat orange / red / brown.
   Yellow is the signal wire (it meets the servo's orange). `cad_wire_paths.md` section 5 already notes a problem with the
   conductor order of this chain in the CAD.
8. **Flags** are not in the CAD. The "10" flag is the only thing that tells the student which row the plug goes on.
9. **The three claw screws are pivots** (2.8 mm clearance in the strip, 2.3 mm thread in the clip, by the CAD). On video each
   is driven until the head is down, with no visible backing-off, and the claw still tilts freely afterwards (5:34, 7:00-7:03).
   "Tighten, then check it still swings" is the most the video supports.
10. **The -Z screw needs the model turned round.** The driver always comes from the builder's right; the robot is turned end
    for end between the +Z screws and the -Z screw. In a fixed-camera guide the third screw comes from the far side.
11. **Hidden by hands:** the moment the plug passes each hole of A6 on the far side, the plug going onto the shield pins,
    and the three seconds in which the join is pushed down behind R4.
12. **Nothing at the very end:** no cable tidy other than item 21, no power, no test, no servo centring. The jaws stay closed
    throughout; the horn was set in stretch 1 and is not touched here.
